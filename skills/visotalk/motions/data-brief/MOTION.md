# Data Brief

Every scene a numbered exhibit from a statistical brief, read aloud. One cobalt for what is being read; the rest is ink and grey.

## 1. Essence, and what it is not

- An exhibit, not a slide: FIG 03 in mono, a headline stating the finding, a unit line, and a source line under the figure, always.
- Real charts: gridlines carrying their figures, ticks, a zero rule, scales chosen for the data, decimals as the data has them.
- Series and shares named where they are, at a line's end or over a segment: no legends.
- The reading guide: a translucent cobalt strip with two ruled edges and a tab, laid on the year, row or phrase being said and slid to the next. What lies under it is the one highlight; the rest is grey.
- A flat, cool plot field with a hairline margin down the left.

Closest is Red Pencil, whose pencil corrects a printed page; here nothing is by hand, the guide only reads, and a chart on real axes is every scene's ground, as in no other built-in motion. Not Stage Light, not a dashboard (one exhibit per scene).

## 2. Ground and material

`bg`, a cool off-white field: no texture, gradient, shadow or card. Exhibit or Cover draws it on every scene; the rest draw on it, never on a panel. Rules make the structure: 3 px ink under a head, 1.5 px `border` gridlines and row rules. Bars are flat fills with square ends.

## 3. Colour roles

- `data` (cobalt): the one thing being read (the guide, what lies under it, its figure), the exhibit's mark, the caption's rule.
- `faint`: every mark not being read. `panel2`: a second grey, an empty track.
- `text`: headlines, labels, table figures, rules. `muted`: units, ticks, sources.
- `good`, `warn`, `bad`: a register's status squares only. A heatmap shades in `text` over `panel`; cobalt only frames.
- Never two highlights, never a colour per series.

## 4. Type and captions

Hanken Grotesk for headlines (62 px, one line, smaller before it wraps), labels (28 to 34 px) and the big reading (150 to 300 px, light, decimals in cobalt). JetBrains Mono for ticks, readings, stamps and code (26 to 30 px). Chinese: Noto Sans SC for both. Never force capitals on content (O(n log n), COVID-19). Captions are a figure's caption: 34 px ink, no box, under a short cobalt rule.

## 5. Motion

Quick and exact, then still. The exhibit opens (headline up 14 px over 0.7 s, source rule drawn); the figure is drawn on its words: lines run, columns grow from zero, a reading counts with its decimals, entries set one by one. The guide moves on the word naming the next value, 0.6 to 1 s, in-out quartic, then rests. Labels land with their marks and stay. Nothing bounces, spins or scales; cuts are hard.

## 6. Composition and framing

Content in x 120 to 1800, headline at y 108, the figure in y 260 to 820, the source at 838. Labels stay in the figure's box, off every mark; charts scale to their data (labels thin, rows shrink).

## 7. Sound

A soft tick as a mark lands, a short slide as the guide moves, dry taps as a figure counts, a paper cut between exhibits, low under the voice.

## 9. Native moves

- **Slide the guide to the value.** On the word, the strip slides to the value now said, which turns cobalt. *Fits content like:* the year a series peaked; the tenth cause in a ranking; the gap row of a table.
- **Draw it as it is said.** A line runs to the period named; columns grow from zero. *Fits content like:* a decade of prices; eight quarters of revenue; a fall and a recovery.
- **Count with every decimal.** The reading climbs to its value, decimals in cobalt, past its references. *Fits content like:* 78.4 years; 40.9 percent; 3,090,964.
- **Name it at its end.** Series and shares carry names and values where they end. *Fits content like:* three cities' prices; a brand's share; women and men.
- **Lay the guide on the phrase.** In a definition or a quote, the guide lies on the words that matter. *Fits content like:* what a measure leaves out; the clause that decides; the loop in a snippet.
- **Fill the register.** Entries set one by one under the guide; those read stay, greyed. *Fits content like:* four findings; five timestamped events; milestones with their states.
- **Pass the highlight.** One item takes the cobalt from another; the rest stay grey. *Fits content like:* one line of a slope; one cause of five; one plan.

## 10. Pitfalls

- A headline that names the topic (Life expectancy) or two findings. Fix: one finding, as a sentence, with its number.
- A source left off because the data is made up. Fix: say so on the source line (Example data).
- A bar chart cut above zero. Fix: bars start at zero; a line may not, and a reading's cut scale is drawn broken.
- Rounding a figure the narration says with decimals. Fix: set `decimals` to what is said.
- Everything in cobalt. Fix: one value per moment; move the guide instead.
- A pie of seven shares, or two years in pies. Fix: a donut holds five at most; past that, the split bar.

## 11. Freedom in each work

Each film chooses its data, its exhibits and their order, whether there is a cover, a method box or a summary register, and its ending. Three far-apart structures: a quarterly report ending on a register of risks with owners; a data lesson whose register gains one line per exhibit, recapped at the end; one long series with the guide sliding through its five turning points.
