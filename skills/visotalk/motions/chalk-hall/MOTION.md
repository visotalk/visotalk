# Chalk Hall

A classroom blackboard where the lesson is chalked as it is said, the result boxed in yellow; a full board is wiped column by column, and the ghost of the last lesson stays under the next.

## 1. Essence, and what it is not

- A matte green-black slate lit from above, hazed by earlier wipes, the chalk tray at its foot.
- Large, legible chalk handwriting, written stroke by stroke on the word.
- Four chalks, each with one meaning: white states and draws, pink is what is given or substituted, yellow is the result (boxed), blue is construction (axes' guides, reasons, helpers).
- The board accumulates: a scene adds to what is there until it is wiped; nothing slides, fades or is typeset.

Closest is **Lab Notebook** (light squared paper, ink and red pen, nothing erased): Chalk Hall is dark, codes meaning in four chalks, and wipes its board, leaving a ghost. Not **Drafting Table** (ruler-true white line on a blue technical sheet): no line here is ruler-true, and it draws the classroom's kit of formulas, axes, tables and textbook symbols.

## 2. Ground and material

`board.tsx` draws the slate: light from above (`panel` to `bg`, `panel2` at the edges), mottle, tooth, swaths of earlier wipes (`ghost`), remnants, and the tray below the caption band. Chalk is solid at its core, broken by the tooth where it thins, with faint powder; a wipe smears what it lifts.

## 3. Colour roles

- `text`: white chalk, everything stated: words, working, figures, axes.
- `op`: pink, what is given or substituted into a formula, and the one deciding word.
- `grad`: yellow, the result: the last line of working (boxed), the value that answers the question. One yellow result per board.
- `data`: blue, construction: dashed guides, reasons beside a step, helper grids, a definition's gloss.
- `muted`: faded chalk: the date, the lesson number, an aside.
- `bg`, `panel`, `panel2`, `border`, `faint`: the slate, never drawn with.

## 4. Type and captions

The chalk hand (`parts/hand.ts`) writes every word stroke by stroke, in writing order, from the single-line strokes of EMS Tech (SIL OFL, `assets/OFL.txt`), each letter a little off true; a 1 has its flag, an I its bars, ² is raised. Chinese is written in stroke order when the build has its strokes, else whole in LXGW WenKai. Headings 60 to 72 px, working 64 to 90, labels 44 to 60, never under 30. Captions: Atkinson Hyperlegible Next and Noto Sans SC (`sans`), 34 px, on slate shadow under a yellow chalk stroke.

## 5. Motion

Writing is the motion: strokes in order, the chalk lifted between them, linear, about 0.15 s a word; a line or shape over 0.6 to 1.6 s, easing in and out; a mark (underline 0.3 to 0.4 s, box or ring 0.5 to 0.7 s) after its words. The wipe takes 2 to 3 s over the sentence that turns; the next heading follows the eraser. Nothing written moves; the board never moves.

## 6. Composition and framing

The board fills the frame and stays. Heading top left, aside small at top right; then the figure or graph on one side, filling it, the working or table on the other. Labels sit beside what they name; leave room for the result box. Keep 60 px from the edges, above y 900.

## 7. Sound

Real chalk in `assets/` (CREDITS.md), copied to the work's `media/`: `chalk-tap` as it lands, `chalk-write-a`/`b`/`c` in turn under words, `chalk-line` under a line, `eraser` under the wipe, cut with `dur` to the move, 12 dB under the voice.

## 9. Native moves

- **Work it line under line.** The formula, the values substituted in pink, the arithmetic, the result in yellow boxed on the word that names it. *Fits content like:* Ohm's law with numbers; solving a quadratic; a percentage change.
- **Plot it as you say it.** Axes chalked with their numbers, points plotted one by one, the curve or bars drawn through them, a blue guide dashed and named. *Fits content like:* a parabola and its vertex; speed against time; births by year.
- **Build the figure piece by piece.** Wires, then the cell, the resistor and the meters, each on its word; a right-angle mark and side names on a triangle. *Fits content like:* a series circuit; a right triangle; a labelled cell.
- **Fill the table, then use it.** The rules and names, values filled column by column as they are worked out, then plotted or compared. *Fits content like:* a function's values; a tense across persons; two products against features.
- **Mark the word.** An underline, box, ring, tick or cross chalked over what decides it. *Fits content like:* the deciding word; a term that cancels; a wrong step.
- **Wipe to the next idea.** The eraser sweeps down and up in columns, leaving streaks and a ghost. *Fits content like:* a worked example cleared; a wrong idea wiped; a new chapter.

## 10. Pitfalls

- Text appearing at once reads as a slide. Fix: tween `write` across its words.
- Too many yellows. Fix: one boxed result per board; pink for inputs, never decoration.
- A board crowded to its edges. Fix: wipe and start a new one rather than shrink the writing.
- Boxes sized by character count. Fix: `measure` from `hand.ts`.
- Forced capitals, or a spoken decimal rounded. Fix: write it as said.
- Crowded ticks. Fix: `step` for five to eight, the range a little past the data.

## 11. Freedom in each work

Each film decides its story, boards, chalks, moves and ending. Three far-apart structures: one board filling across the film to its boxed result; a problem, a wrong attempt crossed out and wiped, then the right working; a run of short boards, each a rule then its example, wiped between.
