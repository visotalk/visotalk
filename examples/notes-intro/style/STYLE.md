## 1. Identity and boundaries
A calm, legible editorial film that makes a quantity or mechanism understandable. Warm paper, ink, restrained color and meaningful motion. Avoid dashboard cards, ornamental gradients and a title-slide sequence. Topic, story, layout and shot count are decisions for each work.

## 2. Color meaning
Use the local style.json palette. Blue identifies the subject being explained; green means saved or gained, red means spent or lost, amber means a condition or uncertainty. Do not change a color's meaning between scenes. Shape and labels must carry the same information without color.

## 3. Type and captions
Inter for prose and labels; JetBrains Mono for figures and code identifiers. Use a small number of clear size levels, with the main quantity dominating. Keep short labels in the drawing and full sentences in the narration. Reserve y >= 900 on a 1920 x 1080 canvas for captions.

## 4. Numbers, currency and units
Every displayed value carries its unit and scope. Speak amounts naturally and display compact digits in labels or captions. Keep hypothetical inputs labelled as such. Compare like units and comparable periods: convert to a common period before drawing magnitude bars against one another. A rate and a total are different quantities; show their relationship as an equation or process when no common-period comparison is established. A sampled frame after the spoken amount must show that amount, not an initial counter value.

## 5. Charts and annotation
Draw the quantities or relations the sentence explains, with explicit labels. Use a true zero for bars and state the time or unit of an axis. Stop at a named threshold while the sentence explains equality; a later increase belongs to its own later sentence. Label each colored area with the quantity it represents. Avoid generic charts when the subject is a mechanism.

For relation diagrams, distinguish an object, its named pointer, and an edge between objects. A pointer visibly moves when the narration says it moves. An edge that means a parent/reference keeps that meaning and arrow direction throughout; history progression needs a different encoding. A join shows its resulting object and the names that now point to it. Route connectors around labels and other nodes, and make a changed state readable by the end of its sentence. These are diagram conventions, not a prescribed scene layout.

## 6. Motion
An object arrives on the word that names it. A growing quantity grows; a pointer that moves really moves. Use deliberate, restrained easing rather than bouncing. Keep each meaningful state visible long enough to understand. A reveal is not a highlight of something shown early.

## 9. Signature moves
A measured line can grow toward a labelled threshold when the content compares cumulative quantities. A pointer can travel to an existing object when the content explains reference or ownership. A small rule may reveal the topic title. Choose by the content; never force all moves into every work.

## 10. Pitfalls
Do not expose all steps of the mechanism before the corresponding words. Do not use the syntax reference as a story template. Do not force unrelated mechanisms into the same chart or composition. Keep explanatory sentences in captions rather than shrink them into the drawing. Do not manufacture evidence, reviewer scores or a pass verdict. Zero QA findings do not establish correct quantities or references: inspect the state at each relevant spoken word.
